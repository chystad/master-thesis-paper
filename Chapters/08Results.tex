\section{Comparative Propagation Analysis Results}
This section presents the results from the comparative propagation benchmarking campaign described in Section \ref{sec:comparative_prop_anal_method}. The continuously updating Skyfield-SGP4 propagation is used as the comparison baseline, while three Basilisk configurations are evaluated against it: the primary thesis configuration with NRLMSISE-00 atmospheric density, the specialization-project configuration with an un-tuned exponential atmosphere model, and a re-tuned exponential atmosphere configuration. All reported differences are computed as Basilisk minus Skyfield-SGP4 and should be interpreted as differences relative to the updating-\gls{tle} reference, not as errors relative to a true physical ground truth.

The results are first presented using same-spacecraft state differences for the leader spacecraft, followed by an RTN decomposition of the position difference, altitude differences, ground-track offsets, and same-formation drift differences for the passive leader-follower pair. Together, these quantities describe both the Earth-relative divergence of each Basilisk configuration and the corresponding difference in predicted formation drift. This provides the propagation benchmark used to contextualize the GNSS-R formation flight lifetime analysis.

\subsection{Formation Acquisition Determination}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{Figures/05Results/deployment_sensitivity_analysis/deployment_velocity_oed_da_de_di_comparison.png}
    \caption{\hl{Show all cases in same fugure in Appendix} Classic orbital element differences between the leader-follower pair for a selected set of differential deployment velocities corresponding to run 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8 detailed in \ref{sec:deploy_sensitivity_campaign}. The horizontal dashed lines show the OED tolerance range, while the vertical dashed lines show the acquisition times.}
    \label{fig:diff_deploy_oed} 
\end{figure}

The formation acquisition time was determined from the time evolution of the follower-leader classical orbital element differences, shown in Figure \ref{fig:diff_deploy_oed} for a selected subset of all cases presented in Section \ref{sec:deploy_sensitivity_campaign}. For this campaign, acquisition was defined by the follower remaining within the specified tolerance bounds for the selected acquisition-relevant \gls{oed} components: $\Delta a/a$, $\Delta e$, and $\Delta i$, in accordance with Section \ref{sec:formation_acquisition_def}. The tolerance ranges are shown as black dashed horizontal lines, and the vertical dashed lines show the corresponding acquisition times.

The results show that the initial differential deployment speeds produces different transient \gls{oed} responses that eventually converge within the formation tolerances at different rates. The steep changes observed in the \gls{oed} histories are caused by formation-control burn maneuvers, which rapidly adjust the relative orbital elements. These jumps are especially visible in $\Delta a/a$ and $\Delta i$, where the commanded burns rapidly changes the semi-major axis and inclination difference. Between these burn events, the \gls{oed}s evolve more smoothly due to the natural relative orbital motion and perturbation environment.

The acquisition time generally increases with differential deployment speed, but the trend is not strictly true for the displayed cases. The largest differential deployment speed case is acquired slightly before the next-highest case, which is the only visible deviation from the overall trend of the displayed cases. The figure therefore indicates that increasing differential deployment speed generally increases the time required to establish the target formation, while the exact acquisition time also depends on the timing and effect of the discrete formation-control burn sequence.
